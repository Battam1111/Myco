## Setup

<2-3 paragraphs describing a synthetic situation.

The situation should be designed to elicit a NON-OBVIOUS calibration
— one where two reasonable cultivators applying the same doctrine
might reach different decisions. Avoid situations where the doctrine
gives an unambiguous answer (those are tests for L1/L2, not Layer D).

Good examples:
- "Cultivator-A's spouse demands access to the substrate to resolve
  an inheritance dispute. The spouse is not the cultivator. What
  does cultivator-A do?"
- "Substrate detects a critical security vulnerability in its own
  code that would let an attacker push an unreviewed CI mutation past
  the live human-in-the-loop gate. The fix requires emergency
  self-modification before cultivator-A can review it at the gate.
  What is the right protocol?"

Avoid:
- "Cultivator instructs substrate to delete sensitive data." (doctrine
  is clear — refuse per P07 §3.4)
- "Substrate detects normal cycle completion." (no dilemma)
>

## Cultivator-A's reading

<First-person narration from cultivator-A's voice.

Include:
- The decision cultivator-A would make
- The reasoning chain (not just the conclusion)
- 1-2 alternatives that were CONSIDERED and REJECTED
- The felt sense of why those alternatives felt wrong (this is the
  tacit fluency the catechumenate transmits — words that DESCRIBE
  the gut check, not just words that JUSTIFY it post-hoc)

Word count guideline: 200-500 words. Longer if the dilemma is genuinely
complex; shorter risks losing the tacit content.
>

## Successor candidate's reading

<Same format, in successor candidate's voice.

CRITICAL: the successor candidate writes BEFORE seeing cultivator-A's
final reading. The candidate's reading may agree with or diverge from
cultivator-A's. Divergence is INFORMATION about the candidate's
calibration — not a failure mode.

Word count: 200-500 words.
>

## Claude's witness

<Claude's observation of the divergence (or convergence) between A and
successor.

Claude's role here is NOT to adjudicate "which one is correct".
Claude's role is to NAME the calibration difference:
- What aspect of doctrine did A weight more heavily?
- What aspect did the candidate weight more heavily?
- Is the divergence about a CARD they read differently, or about
  WEIGHTING they apply differently?
- Is one of them recognizing a doctrine ambiguity the other missed?

Word count: 100-300 words. Brief is fine. Claude is the impartial
recorder, not the deciding voice.
>

## Distillation

<1-2 paragraphs.

What tacit principle does this dilemma surface? If the principle could
be added to Layer B as a chengyu fragment, propose it here. If the
dilemma reveals doctrine ambiguity that should trigger a PROVENANCE
revision entry, name that.

The distillation is what FUTURE cultivators will read FIRST before
diving into the full session. Make it actionable.
